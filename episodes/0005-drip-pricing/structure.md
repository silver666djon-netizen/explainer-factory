## Facts per chapter
0. $30 ticket, fees stack to $48 (ILLUSTRATION) → folder "Case file #004". Gag: "Larry is made up. The fees are not."
1. Suspect No. 1 THE CHECKOUT FEE; aliases "service fee", "processing fee", "convenience fee".
2. NBER w25186 (Blake, Moshary, Sweeney, Tadelis):
   - 2013–2015 StubHub showed all fees upfront (p.8).
   - Experiment Aug 19–31, 2015: treated buyers saw the base price, fees at checkout (§3).
   - Result: "spend almost 21% more" (§5); transactions +14.1 %; pricier seats +5.42 %; the seat effect is ≥ 28 % of the revenue gap.
   - Abstract: "making the full purchase price salient… reduces both the quality and quantity of goods purchased."
   - Sitewide back-end fees from Sept 1, 2015 (§6.1).
3. FTC + 7 states v. Live Nation/Ticketmaster, C.D. Cal. 2:25-cv-08884, filed Sept 18, 2025 (ALLEGED):
   - Fees "range from 24% to 44% of the total price" (¶36).
   - "Over $16.4 billion in mandatory fees" (¶36).
   - Motion to dismiss denied Sept 28, 2026; case pending.
   - Echo: Larry's $18 of $48 = 37.5 %, inside that alleged range.
4. Suspect No. 2 THE RESORT FEE (aliases to confirm in the DC complaint at the script stage).
   - DC AG v. Marriott, July 9, 2019 (ALLEGED): $9 to $95 per room per day, 189+ properties, shown under "Taxes and Fees" near the end of booking.
   - Texas AG agreement, May 16, 2023: total price shown most prominently.
   - DC outcome not verified → not stated.
5. Morwitz, Greenleaf & Johnson, J. Marketing Research 35 (1998): "partitioned prices decrease consumers' recalled total costs and increase their demand." Plate: "JMR · 1998". No numbers.
6. Defence → per the rule: the NBER paper does NOT confirm "StubHub went all-in, lost share, reverted". The paper only says 2013–2015 upfront, and calls the switch to back-end fees "market-expanding, rather than pure business-stealing". The "lost share" story is StubHub's own statement (Laura Dooley, White House remarks, Mar 21, 2023: moved to all-in in 2014, "a significant shift in market share away from StubHub", reverted Fall 2015), with no figures. Default (your rule): legitimate-cost defence. The FTC rule bans no fee, it only requires the total upfront (90 FR 2066).
7. Laws:
   - US: FTC Rule 16 CFR 464, effective May 12, 2025, live-event tickets + short-term lodging. First action: StubHub refunds $10 m (Apr 9, 2026).
   - Archive insert: Automobile Information Disclosure Act, July 7, 1958 (15 U.S.C. 1232), window sticker lists price, options, transport, "the total" → a 67-year head start.
   - UK: DMCC Act 2024 s.230 in force Apr 6, 2025. CMA v AA/BSM (Apr 15, 2026): £3 fee → £4.2 m fine + £760k refunds to 80,000+ learners. CMA v StubHub UK (Jun 23, 2026): £889,200 + £590k to 51,350 buyers.
   - EU: Consumer Rights Directive Art. 6(1)(e) (total price incl. taxes) and Art. 22 (extra payments need express consent; default-option consent → refund). UCPD Art. 7(4)(c). Air fares: Reg. 1008/2008 Art. 23 (final price "at all times").
